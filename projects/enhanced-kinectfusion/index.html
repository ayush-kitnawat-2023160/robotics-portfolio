01 / THE BIG PICTURE

The problem & our solution.

A 3D reconstruction project focused on creating more consistent models from a moving RGB-D camera.

THE REAL-WORLD PROBLEM

Why was this needed?

3D scans are useful for digital twins, inspection and robot mapping, but camera-motion errors and noisy depth can produce broken or distorted models. A reconstruction pipeline must estimate camera movement accurately before combining frames.

THE PROJECT GOAL

What did we set out to build?

Improve the KinectFusion pipeline using visual feature matching to initialize camera motion, ICP to refine alignment, and confidence checks to reduce the influence of unreliable frames.

PRACTICAL VALUE

Why does it matter?

A more stable reconstruction pipeline can reduce manual cleanup and make camera-based scene capture more useful. Our reported TUM RGB-D reconstruction RMSE decreased from 1.20 m to 0.27 m.

04 / THE ENGINEERING

Challenges & what I did.

What made the project difficult, and the choices I made while working through it.

01 / TRACKING

ICP alone is sensitive to initialization

THE PROBLEM

Point-to-plane ICP can converge to a poor alignment when the camera moves too far or the depth geometry is ambiguous.

MY APPROACH

Used learned RGB feature matches and a PnP warm start so ICP begins closer to the expected inter-frame transform.

Evidence / boundary: The repository documents feature warm-start controls and a pure-ICP ablation switch.

02 / DEPTH

Not every RGB-D frame deserves to be fused

THE PROBLEM

Incomplete or noisy depth can damage the integrated surface and accumulate reconstruction errors.

MY APPROACH

Experimented with depth completion and confidence-based frame selection, then evaluated reconstructed geometry instead of relying on attractive renders alone.

Evidence / boundary: Project repository confidence plot and benchmark summary.

03 / DRIFT

Small pose errors become large 3D artifacts

THE PROBLEM

Frame-to-frame errors propagate into TSDF fusion and can distort rooms and surfaces.

MY APPROACH

Combined visual correspondences, multi-scale geometric refinement and loop-consistency experiments to constrain drift.

Evidence / boundary: Reported TUM RGB-D RMSE improvement on the project resume.
