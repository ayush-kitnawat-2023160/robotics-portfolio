Autonomous golf cart localization.

Making a campus vehicle understand where it is — and recognizing when its estimate cannot be trusted.

01 / THE BIG PICTURE

The problem & our solution.

A research project to help an autonomous campus golf cart estimate its position reliably, even when individual sensors become less dependable.

THE REAL-WORLD PROBLEM

Why was this needed?

A self-driving golf cart must know where it is before it can plan a safe route. On campus, trees, buildings, pedestrians and changing light can cause localization to drift or fail. An incorrect position could lead to unsafe decisions.

THE PROJECT GOAL

What did we set out to build?

Build and evaluate a localization system that combines short-term motion estimates with position checks against a campus map, while detecting when its estimate should not be trusted.

PRACTICAL VALUE

Why does it matter?

More dependable localization is a foundation for campus autonomy. This work is still being validated on recorded sensor data; it is not presented as an approved autonomous-driving safety system.

⌖
Project context

Real-sensor recordings and ROS-based experiments; offline and shadow-mode engineering, not a claim of validated autonomous fallback driving.

02 / OWNERSHIP

What I worked on.

My contribution to the project, separate from the broader team's work.

01

Compared LVI-SAM, FAST-LIO2, FAST-LIVO2, ORB-SLAM3 and related sensor pipelines for trajectory drift, robustness and recovery behavior.

02

Worked on synchronization, coordinate frames, map/odom separation, trajectory alignment and 3D LiDAR mapping.

03

Developed and tested map-referenced LiDAR localization ideas using GPS-proximal map tiles and local scan correspondence, rather than treating local odometry as global pose.

04

Investigated visual localization / camera odometry paths and sensor-health signals as candidates for graceful degradation when primary sensing becomes unreliable.

03 / SYSTEM DESIGN

How it works.

01

Observe

Receive timestamped LiDAR scans, camera frames, inertial data and GPS in known sensor frames.

02

Estimate motion

Run a LiDAR-inertial estimator such as FAST-LIVO2 for continuous local odometry.

03

Anchor to the map

Use map-referenced scan matching, nearby map tiles and validation gates to estimate map-frame alignment.

04

Check confidence

Compare sensor health, pose freshness and consistency before trusting corrections or proposing fallback behavior.

04 / THE ENGINEERING

Challenges & what I did.

What made the project difficult, and the choices I made while working through it.

01 / SENSOR REALITY

A sensor works — until conditions change

THE PROBLEM

Visual tracking can degrade in low light, under bridges or when the camera sees mostly repetitive road texture; LiDAR-inertial estimates may also drift or deteriorate.

MY APPROACH

I compared algorithms on the same recordings, inspected drift and failure sequences, and separated short-term odometry quality from long-term map localization. The aim is to detect failure rather than quietly accept a bad pose.

Evidence / boundary: Comparative trajectory and visual/LiDAR diagnostic plots below.

02 / GEOMETRY

Frames and map assumptions can invalidate good estimates

THE PROBLEM

A different LiDAR mounting orientation or an incorrect transform can make old maps and new scans inconsistent. A delayed pose update can produce a similar visible mismatch.

MY APPROACH

I checked extrinsic conventions, transforms and timing separately, and treated mounting changes and delayed updates as distinct causes to be diagnosed — not one catch-all explanation.

Evidence / boundary: Transformation/calibration checks and timed pose diagnostics in the research workspace.

03 / RECOVERY

Continuous odometry is not global localization

THE PROBLEM

A vehicle can keep moving smoothly in odom while losing reliable alignment to the campus map.

MY APPROACH

I worked with a local-odometry plus map-relocalization separation: GPS narrows candidate map tiles, then scan correspondences provide candidate map alignment subject to geometric validation.

Evidence / boundary: Recorded map overview and trajectory overlays; ongoing validation before vehicle control.
